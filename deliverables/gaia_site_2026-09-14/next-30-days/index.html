Two honest conclusions. The rules now hold: nothing above the grade you set, nobody without a stated address, nobody without Engineers Ireland chartership reaches a card. And the pool is thin: the public sources are senior by construction, so reaching the 8 to 15 year grade needs a licensed people-data source, which is a paid step not yet taken.

What this is, in one line each

  • A check. Any list of names, from Recruit CRM, LinkedIn Recruiter or a consultant's notebook, comes back with a verdict per name and the quote behind it. Isadora runs it without you.
  • A map. Every chartered engineer in your niches that the Irish public record can reach, kept current, so a shortlist exists within an hour of a job arriving. This is the part that compounds; a check on its own does not.
  • A reason to call. The project they gave evidence on, the scheme, the firm move, from a named consultant's own seat. That is what moves a reply rate off three percent, and it is measured, not promised.

What it is not

  • Not a replacement for Recruit CRM, LinkedIn Recruiter, or what Maddie built. It feeds all three.
  • Not something that contacts candidates. Your consultants do.
  • Not a score. A name meets the brief or it does not, and the page says why.

What Gaia keeps

  • The map and every card, as files you can open without me.
  • The check, as code, open to Maddie. If she wants to run it inside her dashboard, that is the intended home.
  • Every candidate record in Recruit CRM, with its evidence note, with no per-seat fee.
Three things I need from you. The seniority ceiling and the Ireland rule, one line each. Which live role. And for Maddie, which Recruit CRM plan you are on, since the API sits on Business and above.
