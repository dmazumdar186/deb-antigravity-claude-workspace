# Will these cards stop a scroll? Panel verdict and redesign spec — 2026-10-25

Panel: Hormozi (offer), Saraev (automation maturity), Karpathy (measurement), Murati (what the buyer sees), plus three Fortune 50 executives (LinkedIn sales enablement, brand advertising / ex-ECD, digital demand gen). Evidence: `docs/reference/linkedin_scroll_stop_evidence_2026-10-25.md` (about 80 sourced rows). Lens detail: `docs/reference/linkedin_panel_lenses_2026-10-25.md`. Jev scored the ten headlines.

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph
Your instinct is right and the plan as stated does not work. Hashtags do not bring traffic. The company page hop loses reach. A typographic card with no named buyer, no face and no number is the weakest measured image class, and our ten cards score "generic" on Jev. Nothing guarantees curiosity; what the evidence supports is a different mechanism: recognition of a specific failure, a verifiable artefact, repeated exposure, and a comment and DM routine that converts the attention the card earns.

## 4. Jev on our ten headlines (0–4 stop scale, probabilities)
Stop 2.07–2.75 ("stops the reader sometimes"). Self-relevance ("this is my problem") 0.19–0.46. Generic 0.60–0.78 for nine of ten; only week 08 reads as specific. Panel agrees: the top third of every card is blank paper plus logo, so the feed crop lands on nothing.

## 5. Quantitative redesign spec (follows from sections 2–4)
1. Hook in the top 20% of the image. Headline ≤7–9 words, ≥96 px at 1080 (≥35 px at a 400 px thumbnail), two lines maximum.
2. Name the reader or their situation in the first six words on at least 7 of 10 cards.
3. One verifiable artefact per card: a real screenshot, log line, spreadsheet cell or number with its denominator. No decorative diagrams.
4. Your face on at least 1 in 4 cards; a hand-made mark (your handwriting, a photographed note) on the rest. Zero stock, zero generated imagery.
5. Convert 4 of 10 posts to 5–7 slide document carousels; keep 4:5 for single images.
6. Caption 800–1,000 characters, first line ≤90 characters and carries the claim, closes with a real question. No link, no "comment X", at most 3 hashtags or none.
7. The card carries one fact the caption does not, so dwell happens on both.
8. Series: three recurring layouts at a 70/20/10 mix; variety comes from the artefact, not the grid.
9. Pair every post with 8–12 comments a day on target buyers' posts and a DM reply to every saver and commenter. The card earns the DM; it does not replace it.
10. Landing page is your personal profile. Rewrite headline and Featured before the first post. The ProdCraft page is secondary.
11. Measurement per post: impressions, saves, profile views in 48 h, inbound DMs. After 6 posts, kill a format under 1 save per 500 impressions or under 1% profile-view rate; scale above 2% profile views or 1 inbound DM per 2 posts.
12. Falsifiable prediction: the redesigned cards should beat the current set by ≥30% on profile views per 1,000 impressions and 2× on saves within 4 weeks. If not, the mechanism is wrong, not the polish.

## 6. Honest gaps
No published A/B test of typographic cards vs photos on LinkedIn. No dataset-level consultant funnel; conversion ratios are practitioner ranges. Face-effect figures are low confidence. The October 2026 "AI slop" classifier's full effect is not yet measured. Nothing here guarantees a call; it raises the odds and makes failure measurable.
