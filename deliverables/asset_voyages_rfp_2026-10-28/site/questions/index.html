02What we understood

Your brief lists four expected outcomes. In our words:

  1. More qualified enquiries. The site should bring in real requests for business travel, for events, and for congress flights, not just visits.
  2. Publishing without a developer. Veena and a backup editor should be able to update pages and add congresses themselves.
  3. No loss of Google ranking, no gap online. The pages Google already knows must be carried over carefully, and today’s site stays live until the new one is approved.
  4. Ready for later, not dependent on it. Links with Wondermiles or Air France–KLM can come later. The launch must not wait for them.

Your three activities

  • Business travel for companies, from small firms to travel and purchasing managers.
  • Events (MICE): meetings, incentive trips, conferences and exhibitions, including seminars and group travel.
  • International congresses: helping participants find a congress and request flights to it.

Who visits, and what they should be able to do

  • Small-business owner or office manager: reads the offer for small firms, then asks for an account or a demo.
  • Travel manager: sees your approach and evidence, then requests a review of their travel.
  • HR or communications manager: explores event services, then sends a step-by-step brief and gets a call back.
  • Congress participant: finds the congress, opens its page, and requests a flight quote that ASSET prepares.
  • Existing client: clicks “Client login” and goes straight to the client tool. No password is kept on the website.

03Our recommended approach

A website built for you and owned by you, with its own simple admin, the “ASSET Console”.

  • Our recommendation: a custom build. Your current site runs on WordPress, a sound and widely used choice. For the new portal we recommend a site built for you, with its own simple admin and no paid plugins to keep updated. The audit of your current site (step J1) will confirm whether to keep or replace it, with the costs of each option.
  • Veena publishes on her own. The Console lets her edit pages, add or import congresses from a spreadsheet, and follow every enquiry, without a developer.
  • Everything in ASSET’s name. The domain, the hosting and every account belong to ASSET. You receive the full source and can move to another supplier at any time.

Why this approach

  • Security of your congress codes. The private event codes from Air France–KLM (GM&E codes) stay in the locked admin area and never reach the public site. Fewer moving parts means less to keep secure.
  • Speed on phones. The public pages are prepared in advance and served from close to the visitor, so they open quickly even on a weak mobile connection.
  • Ownership. Nothing is rented from us. If you change supplier, everything goes with you.
  • Low running cost. Roughly the price of a lunch each month (see section 4).

The honest alternative. A ready-made content editor such as Directus, installed on a rented server. It is a sound product and would meet the brief. It does mean a server to rent, update and watch, so more hosting cost and more upkeep each month.

What stays outside the website

  • Issuing tickets, and the flight booking engine. The website collects the request; your team prepares the quote and issues tickets in your usual tools.
  • Advertising campaigns.

What is optional

None of these is needed for launch. Each would be priced separately in the proposal.

  • English version, especially for congress pages.
  • Connection to a customer-relationship tool, so each enquiry lands in your sales system automatically.
  • Wondermiles link-up: for example, one login for clients across the site and the tool, if Wondermiles allows it.
  • AI helper that finds new congresses for your team to check. Nothing is published without a person approving it.

05On availability

Updates go live with no interruption to the site, and any update can be undone instantly. We would target 99.5% availability each month, measured by an independent outside check every 5 minutes and reported to you. Nobody can honestly promise 100%: even the largest hosting providers had outages lasting hours in 2025. What we design for: if one part is down, an enquiry is saved through a backup path and sent on when service returns.

Example outages: 12 June 2025 · 18 November 2025 · 5 December 2025.

06Things we noticed

A few observations from reading your brief and your current site. Most are quick decisions.

  • Client login. Your current client area says “Connexion Traveldoo”, while the brief mentions Wondermiles. Is Wondermiles replacing Traveldoo? (Question 19.)
  • Two domain names. Your site runs on asset-voyagesdaffaires.com; the brief’s letterhead shows assetvoyages.com. We suggest choosing one as the main address and redirecting the other to it, so Google sees one site.
  • Copyright year. The current footer shows both “©2020” and “© 2026”. A small fix, but visitors notice.
  • “Plus de 122 agences”. The Selectour Affaires figure on your site is undated. Your brief asks for claims to be checked before reuse, so we would re-confirm it.
  • The congress catalogue is the biggest new value. It does not exist today. A searchable list, one page per congress, and a flight quote request already filled in with the congress details.
  • Past congresses need a rule. Keep them as an archive, or remove them. Either is fine; we need your choice. (Question 17.)
  • Statistics without a cookie banner. Visitor statistics can work without cookies, which keeps the site lighter and the banner shorter.
  • Today’s site stays live until management approves the new one in writing.

07How the project would run

We follow the seven milestones in your brief. Each one ends with a deliverable and your written approval. Dates go in the proposal.

  1. J0 · KickoffWe agree the team, schedule and responsibilities. From you: access to the current site, hosting and domain, and a list of who owns which account.
  2. J1 · Audit and discoveryWe review the current site and its Google ranking, and confirm the plan and risks. From you: answers to the questions below and a workshop with Veena.
  3. J2 · Look and feelPage layouts and designs for computer and phone. From you: approved photos, logos and brand rules; approval of the designs.
  4. J3 · Core websitePages, Console, forms and statistics on the test copy, open only to your team. From you: French texts (written or approved) and who receives each type of enquiry.
  5. J4 · CongressesThe catalogue, search, congress pages and quote requests, tested. From you: your congress list and the name of the person who approves congresses for sale.
  6. J5 · Before launchOld addresses redirected, tests, fixes, training for Veena and a backup editor, backup and rollback plan. From you: acceptance testing and written go-ahead.
  7. J6 · Settling inMonitoring, final fixes, handover of code, access and exports.

Nothing goes live without written approval from ASSET management. No change to your current site or domain happens before that.

08Our 25 questions

The easiest way to answer: reply by email with the question numbers. Short answers are fine, and “don’t know yet” is a perfectly good answer.

Timing, budget and decisions

  1. 1When is the proposal due, and is there a date the new site has to be live by?
  2. 2Is there a budget range for the core site and the Congress catalogue? A rough bracket is enough.
  3. 3Who at ASSET approves each stage (design, launch), and how many working days should we plan for each approval?

Today’s website and accounts

  1. 4Who manages the current site today (the site software, hosting, domain), and are those accounts in ASSET’s name?
  2. 5Should the new site be on asset-voyagesdaffaires.com, assetvoyages.com, or both?
  3. 6Could you give us read-only access to Google Analytics and Search Console, with a list of any trackers (LinkedIn, Meta…) to keep?
  4. 7Does Selectour ask for anything on your website (logos, legal mentions, brand rules)?

Content and your offer

  1. 8Will your team write the new French copy, or do you want us to rewrite the existing pages? Roughly how many pages do you expect?
  2. 9Do you have approved photos, client logos, testimonials and figures we can publish?
  3. 10For small businesses, is there a defined simplified offer to present? And which assistance services exist today (for example an emergency line, and its hours)?
  4. 11Which journeys should be in English first, and who will translate?

Enquiries and client data

  1. 12Who should receive each type of enquiry (Business Travel, MICE, Congresses), and in which tool: email, or a CRM if you use one?
  2. 13How long may we keep enquiry data, and who handles personal-data requests at ASSET?

Congress catalogue

  1. 14Could you share 10 sample rows from your list of events (the 300 in the brief), with where the listings come from?
  2. 15Who at ASSET approves a congress for sale? Is it one person or several?
  3. 16Once a congress is approved for sale, what can we show publicly about Air France–KLM fares? A “preferential fares” badge, or only “request a quote”?
  4. 17When a congress is over, should its page stay online as an archive, or come down?
  5. 18Should one quote request cover several travellers leaving from different cities (for example, a lab sending doctors from Lyon and Paris)?

Partners and options

  1. 19Is Wondermiles replacing “Connexion Traveldoo”, and is a simple “Client login” link enough at launch? (A technical contact there will help if a single login is wanted later.)
  2. 20For the AI option, what should it do first? Find new congresses for your team to review, or something else?

Security and running the site

  1. 21Does your team sign in with Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace? We’d use it for a secure two-step login to the admin area.
  2. 22Must the site and its data stay in the EU or in France, for example because of your clients’ security questionnaires?
  3. 23Who besides Veena will edit the site day to day? We need to know so we can plan the training.
  4. 24What kind of changes do you expect each month (new pages, offers, congress updates)?
  5. 25What support hours do you need (office hours, or also evenings and weekends in congress season), and how long an interruption can your business accept?
