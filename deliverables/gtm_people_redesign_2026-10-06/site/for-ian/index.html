Just for your eyes · October 2026

For Ian — what changed and why.

The page at this address is a concept on our own domain. Nothing touches gtm-people.com. Every fact on it — the specialisms, the six-step process, the TaaS tiers and fee rules, the 2026 salary bands, the eight FAQs, the 26 partners and the 25 open roles — comes from your live site and its public roles feed, copied verbatim. I invented no quotes, prices, testimonials or article titles.

The one design goal was measurable: ten times less scrolling with zero content lost. Your home page is one long scroll. The concept is a "command deck": a compact hero, then a sticky row of ten tabs that swap one panel in place. Every piece of copy is still in the page (so search engines and Ctrl-F still find it), but a founder reaches pricing, the salary guide or the contact form in one tap with no scrolling at all.

What doesn't exist on your site yet

  • A fee calculator. Base salary + tier + hires per year → the fee per hire against a typical 30% agency fee, and the annual cost including your included placements. It uses your own rules (20 / 17.5 / 15%, £75k and £100k caps). On a £100,000 role at Unicorn it shows £15,000 against £30,000 — your own sentence, made interactive.
  • A roles table with comp filters. All 25 public roles in one dense, sortable table: location, role type, stage, base and OTE in the local currency, work pattern, and the description one tap away. Today a candidate scrolls 5,100 px of cards on a phone to see the same list.
  • Panel deep-links you can paste in LinkedIn DMs. /#pricing, /#salary, /#roles, /#faq open straight on that panel; back and forward work; arrow keys move between tabs.
  • Accordions instead of walls. The six client steps, four candidate steps, eight FAQs and each tier's fine print open on demand, so the page stays short without dropping a word.
  • Partner filter chips (All / For clients / For candidates) over a horizontal snap scroller for the 26 partners and JobSearchBud, with your "Your logo here?" card kept.
  • A phone-first bottom bar (Call · Hire · Roles) and tap-to-call in the header, so the number is never more than one thumb away.
