Just for your eyes · October 2026

For Ian — what changed and why.

The page at this address is a concept on our own domain. Nothing touches gtm-people.com. Every fact on it — the specialisms, the six-step process, the TaaS tiers and fee rules, the 2026 salary bands, the eight FAQs, the 28 partners and the 25 open roles — comes from your live site and its public roles feed, copied verbatim. I invented no quotes, prices, testimonials or article titles.

The design goal was measurable: the same story, with far less on screen at any one moment. Your home page is one long scroll of fifteen different layouts. The concept tells it as a scroll-story — one idea per screen, with pinned sections that swap their content in place as you scroll — so the reader never meets a wall of text, yet nothing is cut.

Two cuts

/ is the scroll-story: one idea per screen; the hero, the specialisms and the open roles are pinned and change under your thumb. /deck/ is the compact tabbed cut: two desktop screens, ten tabs, every panel one tap away. Both carry the same copy. Tell me which direction you'd back and I'll take that one to a finished build.

What doesn't exist on your site yet

  • A pipeline you can scrub. The hero draws 360 approached sellers, maps them, screens them to a shortlist of five and lands one hire as you scroll — your six steps, told without a single paragraph.
  • A fee calculator. Base salary + tier → your fee against a 30% agency fee, using your own rates (20 / 17.5 / 15%). On a £100,000 role at Unicorn it shows £15,000 against £30,000 — your own sentence, made interactive.
  • Open roles as a filtered card stack. All 25 public roles, one in focus at a time, filtered by role type and London / New York / Remote, with base and OTE in the local currency and an equity flag.
  • The salary guide as drawn bars. Six roles, base in violet and OTE in mint, drawn to scale as they enter the screen, with your stage notes underneath.
  • Partner chips (All / For clients / For candidates) over a hover-to-read list of the 28 partners and JobSearchBud, with your "Your logo here?" line kept.
  • A phone-first bottom bar (Call · Hire · Roles) and tap-to-call in the header, so the number is never more than one thumb away.
