What was built

  • Two editions, one codebase. greenjobs.ie and greenjobs.co.uk render from the same templates and scripts with their own data, currency and map. The edition switch keeps you on the same page.
  • Real data only. 98 live roles from 19 employers, 11 sectors with roles, 10 map regions with counts, 56 disclosed salaries. Every job page links to the real listing. No invented testimonials, prices or audience numbers.
  • Search that answers instantly. Type-ahead on the home page, a filter rail with every state in the URL, list / map / saved views, a command palette (Ctrl+K), and per-job pages with JobPosting structured data, which the current site explicitly disables ("Invalid salary data, so Google Schema is disabled").
  • A sector taxonomy that means something. The live site lists 214 sectors, most showing "(0)". The demo derives eleven sectors from what each description actually says, and never shows an empty one.
  • Maps from real geometry. Natural Earth admin-1 boundaries merged into the 26 counties and 12 UK regions at build time, rendered as keyboard-operable buttons.
  • Salary explorer, career compass, "Ask GreenJobs". Charts drawn in-house (no library), a seven-question deterministic quiz whose result is a shareable link, and a smart-match panel that today runs entirely in the browser.
  • Zero third-party requests. Fonts are self-hosted; there is no analytics, no CDN, no framework, no build-time packages. Light and dark themes, reduced-motion fallbacks, WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, 44 px targets, print stylesheet on job pages.

What changed since the first cut

  • A light opening, then the film. The home page opens on a white hero: one headline, the central search (what job, where), the live counts, a large "Post a job" beside "Find a job", the Certified B Corporation mark, and the first eight live roles with a career level read from each title. The pinned, scroll-driven sequence follows as the second section, on paper: the 10-region map with live counts, the disclosed-salary bands, the sector ring. The map points are sampled from the SVG at build time, so the page ships no extra assets and makes no extra requests. Under reduced motion the sequence is a static page with the map copy and every count in reach.
  • "Your fit in ten seconds." On the home and jobs pages: paste a CV or three words and the six closest live roles appear instantly, with the matched words, your position on the board's disclosed-salary strip, a small map of where the matches are, and a caveat. The matcher is the upgraded local one (title-weighted, two-word phrases, sector and place boosts). Every result is a link you can share.
  • The live ad builder. On the Employers page the form renders the exact job card and job-page header as you type, previews a logo without uploading it, and reads reach from this week's data (live roles and median disclosed salary in the chosen sector). Sending still posts nothing and says so.
  • "Where this role sits." Every job page with a published salary shows that range against the board's disclosed distribution, overall and in its sector, with keyboard-reachable marks and a table; roles without one get an honest line with the sector's disclosed range instead. Both show how many similar roles are live in the same region.
  • Motion that explains. Numbers count up as they come into view, sparklines draw in, the map colours in region by region, a job title morphs from card to page and back, a thin bar in the header shows how far down you are. All of it is off under reduced motion and none of it moves layout.

Proposed for phase two

  • Migration off the current vendor. The live boards run on a 2013-era hosted platform (jQuery 1.11, Bootstrap 3, IE8 shims, classic ASP). The demo's build already renders every page statically from JSON; the next step is a small admin that writes that JSON, and a nightly build and deploy. Cloudflare Pages hosting is free at this size.
  • Applicant tracking. Apply-in-place with a per-employer inbox, status changes and email notifications, replacing the redirect to a third-party form.
  • Employer self-serve. The "Post a job" preview on the Employers page becomes real: draft, preview, pay, publish, with the posting distributed across the network sites as today.
  • Model-backed assistant (optional, last). "Ask GreenJobs" keeps its interface and its contract (a query in, ranked role IDs and a one-line "why" out). The local TF-IDF matcher is swapped for a hosted language model behind a small Cloudflare function, with the key held server-side and never in the page; the vendor is chosen at phase two. The function stub, prompt contract and rate limit are already written. Cost model: a query sends the question plus the compact live dataset (about 98 roles at roughly 120 tokens each, so about 12k tokens) and returns about 300 tokens. Multiply (12.3k input + 0.3k output) by the chosen vendor's per-token rate on launch day, then by 1,000, for the cost per 1,000 queries; prompt-cache the dataset portion so repeat queries within the cache window bill only the question. The rate is not quoted here to avoid publishing a stale number.

Notes on honesty

  • Sector tags, region matches and annualised salaries are derived from the listing text by rules that are in the build script; they can be wrong on individual roles and are labelled as derived where they appear.
  • The "Why GreenJobs" facts, the employer offer and the B Corp mark come from your own pages as fetched on 2026-09-22 (brief.md). The wording "Certified B Corporation" was confirmed by the owner on 2026-09-24; the demo shows the mark in the hero, the header and the footer with that wording and adds no certification date, score or other claim.
  • Newsletter, alert and post-a-job forms send nothing and say so.
  • Job descriptions are shown as published by employers (sanitised of scripts and styles); the word-filter that keeps machine vocabulary off the public pages does not apply inside them.
