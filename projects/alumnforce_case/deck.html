Campaign Targeting Assistant
From a sentence to a segment you can trust.

The administrator describes the audience in one sentence. The platform returns a segment with a confidence grade and the filters it used, inside the existing segmenter. No member record ever leaves the platform.

For the administrator

A list in minutes instead of an hour, and a list that is true: every member graded fresh / to verify / stale before the send.

For the client's leadership

Board-grade numbers ("1,200 in finance, 180 in London, 40 % paying") that come with their reliability, and a one-sentence answer for the DPO.

For AlumnForce

A credible first step toward the 2027 "platform that acts on its data", metered per tenant, shippable by 3–4 developers in one quarter, safe for clients who refuse any transfer.

The one-line thesis

The request says speed. The field says trust. The first increment therefore ships data quality and a graded segment first, and uses the language model only for the one thing it is good at: turning a sentence into filters.

2 · The first increment

Principle: a graded segment, not a chatbot

Three deliberate choices

  • Deterministic first. Normalisation, deduplication and freshness grading run in MariaDB/PHP with no model call. They create value for every tenant, including Marc's 400-member club and Philippe's university that will refuse any transfer.
  • The model does one thing. It turns the admin's sentence into a structured filter set, constrained to the segmenter's existing ~60 criteria. It never sees a member.
  • The human keeps the hand. The filters land pre-filled in the segmenter the admin already knows. They edit, see the graded count, then save or send. Nothing is sent automatically.

Why not the chatbot Antoine asked for

A free chat over profiles needs member data in the prompt (DPO veto), has no evaluable output, and sets a "ChatGPT" expectation we cannot meet with 3 developers in a quarter. A constrained assistant with visible filters is evaluable, explainable and reversible.

What the admin sees (target experience)

Types: "anciens de la promo 2015 à 2018 qui bossent dans la finance à Londres"

Gets, in under 5 seconds: the segmenter opened with Promotion: entre 2015 et 2018 · Secteur (Poste actuel): Finance OU Banque OU Finance/Assurance · Ville (Lieu de travail): à proximité de London, 30 km and a count: 72 members — 31 fresh14 to verify27 stale

One click: send to fresh + to-verify, and launch a "please confirm your profile" nudge to the stale 27 through the existing Campagne de mise à jour. The segment is saved with its sentence, its filters and its grades, so Karim can quote it to his board next month.

3 · The orchestration

What goes through the model, and what never does

HumanDeterministic (PHP / MariaDB / OpenSearch / n8n)Language model, via the internal gateway
1 · AdminTypes a sentence in the segmenter. Session carries tenant, role and visible criteria.
→
2 · Pre-flightLength cap, strip emails/phone numbers from the sentence, check tenant flag and per-tenant quota, cache lookup (same sentence, same tenant).
→
3 · Model callInput: the sentence + the schema of the ~43 criteria and operators this admin may use (ET of OU, radius, date bounds) + reference value lists. Output: strict JSON filter set, or "cannot map" with the unmatched part. No member row, ever.
→
4 · ValidatorRejects unknown criteria; maps values to reference tables ("Londres" → London); applies the admin's permission scope; logs tenant, admin, tokens, cost.
→
5 · Query + gradingExisting segmenter engine runs the filters on MariaDB/OpenSearch; each result gets its A/B/C freshness grade.
→
6 · Admin decidesSees filters pre-filled, graded count, the part that could not be mapped. Edits, saves, sends, or nudges the stale ones.

Rights

The assistant runs inside the admin's session: same ACL as the segmenter, criteria they cannot see are not even in the schema sent to the model; results are scoped to their instance by the existing query layer. Nothing new to secure.

Where the human is

Before (writes the sentence), in the middle (sees and edits the filters), after (chooses the action). The model never triggers a send, a merge or a profile edit.

Degraded mode

Gateway down, quota reached, flag off, or model returns "cannot map": the segmenter works exactly as today. The feature is additive.

3 · The orchestration · data, cost, business model

The one-sentence answer for the DPO, and what a use costs

For Thomas (V8) and Philippe (V11), one sentence

"Only the administrator's question and the list of filter names leave the platform, to a model hosted in the EU; never a member's name, e-mail or profile."

Minimised, justified, traceable

  • Minimised: prompt = sentence + schema + reference lists. Pre-flight strips personal identifiers typed in the sentence ("send to marie.dupont@…").
  • Justified: the only purpose is translation into filters; documented in the record of processing.
  • Traceable: every call logged with tenant, admin, timestamp, prompt hash, tokens, model, region; exportable per tenant for a DPO audit.
  • Refusable: tenants who refuse any transfer keep deliverables 1 and 3 (no model) and lose only the sentence box.

Cost of one use (to be confirmed on the gateway in week 1)

~1,500–2,500 input tokens (schema + lists dominate; cacheable prefix), ~150–300 output tokens. On a mid-tier model that is a fraction of a euro cent per request; at 200 requests a month for a heavy tenant, well under one euro. The real cost is engineering, not inference. The cost meter exists because the brief requires knowing which client generates what, and because a runaway loop must be visible the same day.

Business model — my position

Included in the subscription with a fair-use quota per tenant (e.g. 300 requests/month), metered beyond. Catherine (V12) will not pay for "AI"; she will pay for dues collected. Pricing the sentence box would kill adoption before we can measure value. If after the pilot the freshness nudge demonstrably lifts dues, that loop can become a priced option.

9 / 15Provider and region: EU-hosted endpoint through the internal gateway; provider choice stays open and swappable because the contract is "schema in, JSON out".
4 · The measure

Quality, adoption, value — and the criterion that stops it

Quality (offline evals, before any tenant sees it)

  • Golden set of 60–100 real sentences collected with CS from tickets and the verbatims, each with the expected filter set, written by two people independently.
  • Metrics: filter-level precision and recall; exact-match rate; "cannot map" rate on sentences that should fail (e.g. "the ones who look rich").
  • Gate: exact match ≥ 85 %, no false criterion on permission-restricted fields, re-run on every prompt, schema or model change. Results versioned next to the prompt.

Quality (online, in use)

  • Edit rate: share of generated filters the admin changes before saving.
  • Rejection rate: sentence typed, nothing saved.
  • Weekly manual sample of 20 sentences by PM + CS during the pilot.

Adoption

  • Share of new segments created via the sentence box, per tenant.
  • Weekly active admins using it; return use in week 2 and week 4.
  • Excel export share of list-building — should fall (V1, V7).
  • List-related support tickets per 100 campaigns (V9's "one in five").

Value

  • Time from first click to saved segment (baseline measured before launch).
  • "Not me / I moved" replies and unsubscribes per campaign (V3).
  • Share of grade-A profiles per instance, month over month; profile updates triggered by nudges.
  • Response rate on dues campaigns sent to A+B vs historical.

Stop criterion

After 8 weeks on 10 pilot instances: if fewer than 30 % of new segments go through the sentence box, or the edit rate stays above 50 %, or grade-A share has not moved, I stop the model layer and keep the data-quality layer, which will have paid for itself in tickets alone.

10 / 15Baselines are measured in weeks 1–4 on the current segmenter, so the pilot compares against a number, not a feeling (V7).
5 · The risks

Three risks, and my position on each

1 · The data is too dirty for any assistant to look good

69 % of profiles untouched for two years; sectors missing on a third; eight spellings of one city. An assistant on top of this returns wrong lists with confidence, and the brand damage lands on AlumnForce, not on the model.

Position: data quality is the first increment, not a prerequisite someone else owns. Grades make the dirt visible instead of hiding it, and the nudge loop starts cleaning it from day one. I accept shipping the sentence box later than a pure-AI roadmap would.

2 · Personal data and the DPO veto

Philippe (V11) will block any transfer of student data to a US model; several tenants will refuse categorically. One ambiguous answer in a sales call and the feature is dead for that account.

Position: design so the answer is short and true: no member data in any prompt, EU-hosted endpoint, per-call log exportable per tenant, flag per instance. Tenants who still say no get everything except the sentence box. I will not trade this for a richer prompt.

3 · The expectation gap

Antoine (V2) expects ChatGPT. The product is a translator with a fixed vocabulary. If the first demo feels like a dumber chatbot, pilots disengage and Yessica's story repeats (V7).

Position: never call it a chat; always show the filters it produced and the part it could not map; make the first win the graded count, which no chatbot gives them. Onboard the three expert tenants first so the pattern is set by people who know what a filter is.

Watched but not top three: cost runaway (metered and capped per tenant from day one); model-provider lock-in (schema-in/JSON-out contract keeps it swappable); small tenants finding it irrelevant (V6 — the flag stays off for them and they still get the data-health panel).

11 / 15Each position is a trade I am willing to defend, not a mitigation list.

Toward 2027

Why this increment is the right first step for "a platform that acts on its data"

Q2 — Answers for leadership

"How many alumni placed, where, in which sectors" (V10) as three questions over the graded base: every number carries its reliability. Same translator, new output type (aggregate instead of list), on the existing Statistiques module's data, still no member data in the prompt.

Q3 — Automations that keep the base alive

n8n loops: nudge on job-change signals, re-grade after each login, suggest merges. The platform maintains its own freshness instead of the admin.

2027 — Agents on segments

An agent that proposes a dues campaign to the right 180 people, with the list, the grades and the expected response rate, and waits for a human yes. Possible only because the three previous steps made the data trustworthy and the actions auditable.

The thread through all of it

  • Deterministic where the answer must be exact (who is in the list, what it costs, who may see what).
  • A model where language is the input (a sentence, a question).
  • A grade on every output, so the platform can say how much to trust itself.
  • A human before every irreversible action.

What this gives AlumnForce commercially

A true "yes" to "do you do AI?" with a one-sentence data answer; a data-health score every CS can show at renewal; a measured basis to price automation later, instead of pricing a text box now.

Annex B · Selection logic (one page) · "les diplômés travaillant dans la finance à Londres depuis plus de trois ans" · full SQL and 49-row result attached (annexB.sql, annexB_result.csv)
-- Reference date 2026-10-12. Run as written on SQLite, CSV loaded unchanged as `profils`.
-- 0. DEDUPLICATE: file has 525 rows, brief says 500. M0501..M0525 are duplicate persons
--    (capitalised/compound surname, truncated first name, gmail, same job + start date).
CREATE TABLE membres AS
WITH p AS (SELECT *, lower(substr(prenom,1,3)) pn,
            lower(replace(replace(replace(nom,'-',''),' ',''),'.','')) nn FROM profils),
doublons AS (SELECT b.member_id drop_id FROM p a JOIN p b
   ON a.member_id<>b.member_id AND a.pn=b.pn
  AND (instr(a.nn,b.nn)>0 OR instr(b.nn,a.nn)>0)
  AND (a.date_debut_poste=b.date_debut_poste OR a.poste=b.poste)
  AND (a.date_maj_profil>b.date_maj_profil OR (a.date_maj_profil=b.date_maj_profil AND a.member_id<b.member_id)))
SELECT * FROM p WHERE member_id NOT IN (SELECT drop_id FROM doublons);      -- 499 members
-- 1. NORMALISE (deterministic lookup tables, no model, nothing leaves the platform)
CREATE TABLE membres_norm AS SELECT m.*,
 CASE WHEN lower(ville) LIKE '%london%' OR lower(ville) LIKE '%londres%'
       OR lower(ville) LIKE '%canary wharf%' THEN 'London' ELSE ville END ville_norm, -- 8 forms
 CASE WHEN lower(trim(coalesce(pays,''))) IN ('uk','united kingdom','royaume-uni') THEN 'GB'
      WHEN pays IS NULL AND lower(ville) LIKE '%lond%' THEN 'GB' ELSE pays END pays_norm,
 CASE WHEN secteur_declare IN ('Finance','Banque','Finance / Assurance') THEN 'finance:declare'
      WHEN entreprise IN ('Amundi','AXA IM','Barclays','BNP Paribas','Crédit Agricole CIB',
        'Deutsche Bank','Goldman Sachs','HSBC','J.P. Morgan','Lazard','Morgan Stanley',
        'Natixis','Rothschild & Co','Société Générale','UBS') THEN 'finance:entreprise'
      WHEN lower(poste) REGEXP 'financ|m&a|investment bank|ib analyst|trader|quant|portfolio
        |asset manag|opcvm|private equity|equity research|treasury|credit risk|risk manager
        |contrôleur de gestion|fp&a|banquier|conformité bancaire|auditeur financier
        |chargé d.affaires|gestionnaire de portefeuille|directeur financier' THEN 'finance:poste'
      ELSE 'non' END finance_src,
 CASE WHEN date_maj_profil >= date('2026-10-12','-2 years')
       OR derniere_connexion >= date('2026-10-12','-1 year') THEN 'A'      -- fresh
      WHEN date_maj_profil >= date('2026-10-12','-4 years') THEN 'B'       -- to verify
      ELSE 'C' END fraicheur                                                -- stale
FROM membres m;
-- 2. SELECT
SELECT member_id, poste, entreprise, finance_src, fraicheur FROM membres_norm
WHERE ville_norm='London'                                  -- "à Londres"
  AND finance_src<>'non'                                   -- "dans la finance"
  AND date_debut_poste <= date('2026-10-12','-3 years')    -- "depuis plus de trois ans"
  AND promo <= 2026;                       -- "diplômés": all are (promos 2005-2024)

Result

49 members (499 after dedup → 168 in London → 72 in finance → 49 with 3+ years in post). Source: 24 declared sector, 17 employer, 8 job keywords. Freshness: A 1B 5C 43 · 14 paying. Naive filter (ville='Londres', secteur='Finance'): 2. Normalised, no dedup: 26.

Assumptions

  • Reference date 2026-10-12 (latest date in the file: 2026-09-22).
  • "Diplômés" = everyone (promos 2005–2024). The "Master Finance" diploma is not a finance signal: only 21 of its 50 holders work in finance.
  • "Finance" = declared sector, else employer in a finance reference list, else job-title keywords. Keywords are the weakest source: 2 of 8 are doubtful ("Professeur de finance" at Renault, "Assistant administratif et financier" at Orange) and are shown as "to confirm".
  • "3+ years" = current job started on or before 2023-10-12.

What the data cannot verify, and what the product does

  • "In London for 3+ years": no date for the location, only for the job. Job tenure is the proxy, labelled "3+ years in current role" in the UI.
  • "Still true": 43 of 49 profiles are 4+ years old with no login in 2 years. Graded C; a nudge is offered before any send instead of silent inclusion.
  • Duplicates: merges proposed to the admin, never applied silently.
