Trusted Segments
Describe your audience. Get a segment you can trust, without a single member record leaving the platform.

The administrator types one sentence. The platform returns the filters it used, inside the existing segmenter, and a reliability split on every member: current, to confirm, to reactivate. The model only translates the sentence; the data stays home. Proof on their own file: the same sentence returns 2 (naive filter), 26 (normalised), 49 (full logic) members, of whom only 6 are current or to-confirm and worth e-mailing today.

For the administrator

A list in minutes instead of an hour, and a list that is true: the people who moved are flagged before the send, not after.

For the client's leadership

Dues campaigns reach people who still exist; board numbers carry their reliability; one hour per list becomes minutes; the DPO gets a one-sentence answer to instruct on.

For AlumnForce

The first self-funding step toward the 2027 "platform that acts on its data": a renewal argument for 350 tenants, a sales answer to "do you do AI?", metered per tenant.

The thesis, and the difference

The request says speed; eight of twelve verbatims say trust. To my knowledge, competitors sell the chatbot Antoine asked for: a list. We sell a list and how far to trust it, with the one sentence Philippe's DPO accepts. The copyable part is the sentence box. The moat is the normalised, reliability-scored member base per tenant.

2 · The first increment · principle

A reliability split on every segment, not a chatbot

Three deliberate choices

  • Deterministic first. Normalisation, duplicate detection and the reliability split run in MariaDB/PHP with no model call. They create value for every tenant, including Marc's 400-member club and Philippe's university that will refuse any transfer.
  • The model does one thing. It turns the admin's sentence into a structured filter set, constrained to the segmenter's existing criteria. It never sees a member.
  • The human keeps the hand. The filters land pre-filled in the segmenter the admin already knows. They edit, see the split, then save or send. Nothing is sent automatically.

Vocabulary, chosen on purpose

Not "chat", not "grade", not "stale": those read as a reproach to the client's data stewardship. Two names only: the tab is Trusted Segments, the chips are Reliability: CurrentTo confirmTo reactivate. Admin vocabulary never reaches members: on their own profile they see "Profile last updated 4 years ago · Confirm / Correct", one button, no label.

What the admin sees (the demo must end on this)

Types: "alumni from the 2015 to 2018 classes who work in finance in London"

Gets, in under 5 seconds: the segmenter opened with Class year: between 2015 and 2018 · Sector (current job): Finance OR Banking OR Finance/Insurance · City (workplace): within 30 km of London, the part it could not map shown inline under the box ("Add this criterion by hand"), never a blank page, and three clickable chips above the table (illustrative numbers): 72 members — 31 current14 to confirm27 to reactivate

One click: send to current + to-confirm; "Reactivate 27 members" opens a pre-filled Profile Update Campaign (the existing module; fixed, tenant-editable text, no model-written copy) and returns here. The segment is saved as "Finance · London · classes 2015-2018" with the sentence as description, so Karim can quote it to his board next month.

Why not the chatbot Antoine asked for

A free chat over profiles needs member data in the prompt (DPO veto), has no evaluable output, and sets a "ChatGPT" expectation 3 developers cannot meet in a quarter. A constrained assistant with visible filters is evaluable, explainable and reversible.

3 · The orchestration

What goes through the model, and what never does

HumanDeterministic (PHP / MariaDB / OpenSearch)Language model, via the internal gateway
1 · AdminTypes a sentence in the segmenter. Session carries tenant, role and visible criteria.
→
2 · Pre-flightFlag and quota check; length cap; scrub e-mails, phones and exact full-name matches (first + last token) from this tenant's member table; replace any other capitalised two-token sequence by a placeholder the admin sees, with place and sector words allow-listed; log every hit, never block; false-flag rate measured on the eval set; cache lookup.
→
3 · Model callInput: scrubbed sentence + the tenant's full criteria schema (names and operators only, no data) + closed taxonomies only (sector list, country list, diploma types). Output: strict JSON filter set with place text and relative durations, or "cannot map" with the unmatched part. No member row, no employer list, ever.
→
4 · ValidatorRejects unknown criteria; geocodes place text and resolves durations locally; maps free values to reference tables; sanity bounds (radius cap, operator/value types per criterion, result-count vs tenant-size alert); one repair retry then "cannot map"; hard timeout → degraded; logs tenant, admin, tokens, cost.
→
5 · Query + splitExisting segmenter engine runs the filters on MariaDB/OpenSearch; each result carries its reliability status.
→
6 · Admin decidesSees filters pre-filled next to their own sentence (a diff view), the split, the unmapped part inline. Edits, saves, sends, or reactivates.

Rights

The assistant runs inside the admin's session. The model sees the tenant's criteria names, never data, so the cached prefix is shared per tenant; the validator then strips any criterion the role may not use (unit-tested, not left to the model), and results are scoped by the existing query layer, and the cache key includes tenant, taxonomy hash, role and schema hash so no filter set leaks across roles or instances.

Where the human is

Before (writes the sentence), in the middle (sees and edits the filters), after (chooses the action). The model never triggers a send, a merge or a profile edit. Prompt injection in the sentence can produce a rejected filter set or a wrong one (for instance widened to everyone), which the admin sees with its count before any send; widening attacks are in the negative eval set.

Degraded mode

Gateway down, quota reached, flag off, timeout: the segmenter works exactly as today. Partial "cannot map": mapped filters apply, the rest is shown inline, never a blank page. Global kill switch at the gateway, owned by the Lead Developer. The feature is additive.

What the DPO can instruct on, and what a use costs

For Thomas (V8) and Philippe (V11), one sentence

"Only the administrator's question, scrubbed of identifiers and full names, and the names of the filters leave the platform, to a model hosted in the EU under a no-retention, no-training contract. No member profile is ever sent; residual hits in the question are logged and reviewed."

The file behind the sentence (Annex C has the questionnaire rows)

  • Roles: tenant controller, AlumnForce processor, model provider a sub-processor with 30-day notice; DPA with zero retention, no training; EU hosting and jurisdiction stated.
  • DPIA: AlumnForce supplies a screening memo and a balancing-test template; each tenant, as controller, records its own basis and decision; the status can be disabled per tenant.
  • Reliability status is derived personal data. Computed per tenant only after the flag is on, never for all 350 by default. Likely basis: the tenant's legitimate interest in accurate records, the tenant's call. Shown to the member on their profile, contestable in one click (tenant admin answers within 10 business days; a contest freezes the status as "confirmed" for 12 months); template notice clause. Not an export field, though a status-filtered export reveals it, and the notice says so. Reactivation honours opt-outs and the tenant's basis for contacting silent members.
  • Logs: pseudonymised admin, prompt hash and redacted sentence, 12 months, tenant-scoped export, deleted on offboarding; saved sentences follow the same policy.

Cost of one use (confirmed on the gateway in week 1)

~1,500–2,500 input tokens (schema and taxonomies, in a cached prefix), ~150–300 output: a fraction of a euro cent per request on a mid-tier model, under one euro a month for a heavy tenant. Per-admin rate limit, per-tenant quota with soft block and CS alert. The cost that matters is engineering (slide 13).

Provider position

Contract is "schema in, JSON out", so the provider is swappable in days. If today's gateway points to a US provider, the sentence box launches only on an EU-hosted endpoint; the reliability layer does not wait for that decision.

Security questionnaire, ready

One row per item in Annex C (TLS, gateway auth, certifications, residency, pentest scope, incident SLA), so Sales never answers "it's secure". No-transfer tenants keep everything except the sentence box.

4 · The measure

Quality, adoption, value, and one stop criterion per layer (every threshold is an initial guess, reset on the week-4 baseline)

Quality, offline (before any tenant sees it)

  • Golden set of 100 real sentences from tickets and the verbatims, labelled by two people independently; inter-annotator agreement reported. Negative set: sentences that must return "cannot map" (discriminatory, impossible, injection attempts).
  • Metrics: filter-level precision and recall, exact-match rate, "cannot map" accuracy. Gate: exact match ≥ 85 % with its 95 % interval stated (on 100 items, roughly ±7 points), zero unknown criteria or values after validation; role stripping is covered by unit tests, not evals. Re-run on every prompt, schema or model change, and replayed nightly against the live endpoint with an alert on a 5-point drop, because providers update silently; provider model version logged per call.

Quality, online

  • Edit rate (filters changed before saving, per segment), rejection rate (typed, nothing saved, per sentence), cannot-map share (per sentence): all per tenant, aggregate and pseudonymised, disclosed to admins at flag-on. Weekly sample of 20 sentences, never attributed to a person. Edited sentences become eval candidates.

Adoption (per tenant, visible to CS, not only to me)

  • Share of new segments created via the sentence box; weekly active admins; return use in weeks 2 and 4.
  • Excel export share of list-building (should fall); list-related tickets per 100 campaigns (target −30 % on pilot tenants, tagged from week 1).

Value

  • Time from first click to saved segment (baseline weeks 1–4).
  • "Not me / I moved" replies and unsubscribes per campaign (V3).
  • Share of "current" profiles per tenant month over month; profile updates triggered by reactivation campaigns.
  • Response rate on dues campaigns sent to current + to-confirm vs historical.

Stop criteria, one per layer, two dates

  • Sentence box, week 12 (3 pilot weeks, leading indicators): stop if fewer than 30 % of new segments on the 7 non-expert tenants use it, or edit rate stays above 50 %, or the eval gate is not met. Keep the reliability layer.
  • Reactivation loop, week 17 (30 days after the first campaign): stop it if fewer than 10 % of "to reactivate" profiles move to "current". Keep the status and criteria, which cost nothing to run.
  • Expert tenants are reported separately so three power users cannot carry the number.
  • Owners: quota and anomaly alerts → CS on-call, one business day; eval gate or nightly replay failure → PM blocks release, Lead Developer notified; integrity tickets → data owner, weekly.

5 · The risks

Three risks, and my position on each

1 · The data is too dirty for any assistant to look good

68 % of profiles untouched for two years; sectors missing on a third; eight spellings of one city. An assistant on top of this returns wrong lists with confidence, and the brand damage lands on AlumnForce, not on the model.

Position: the reliability layer is the first increment, not a prerequisite someone else owns. The split makes the dirt visible instead of hiding it, and reactivation starts cleaning it from day one. I accept shipping the sentence box later than a pure-AI roadmap would.

2 · Personal data and the DPO veto

Philippe (V11) will block any transfer of student data to a US model; several tenants will refuse categorically; the reliability status itself is profiling if nobody says so. One ambiguous answer in a sales call and the feature is dead for that account.

Position: design so the answer is short and true: scrubbed sentence only, closed taxonomies, EU endpoint under a no-retention contract, status visible and contestable by the member, per-tenant flag and audit export. Tenants who still say no get everything except the sentence box. I will not trade this for a richer prompt.

3 · The expectation gap

Antoine (V2) expects ChatGPT. The product is a translator with a fixed vocabulary. If the first demo feels like a dumber chatbot, pilots disengage and Yessica's story repeats (V7).

Position: never call it a chat; always show the filters and the unmapped part; make the first win the split, which no chatbot gives them; end every demo on "6 current or to-confirm you can e-mail". Onboard the three expert tenants first so the pattern is set by people who know what a filter is.

Watched but not top three: cost runaway (metered, capped, kill switch); provider lock-in (schema-in/JSON-out contract); scope slip (deliverable 3 and the Vue pre-fill are the first to move); small tenants finding it irrelevant (V6: flags off, they still get the duplicate report and a reliability score at renewal).

Money

What pays for the quarter, and what we charge

Three levers, one primary

  • Retention (primary): 350 installed tenants, segmenter abandoned by month six (V7). A reliability score at renewal is a CS argument I have not seen a competitor make.
  • New logo: "do you do AI?" (V8) answered with the sentence on slide 9 and Annex C.
  • Upsell: the reactivation loop, the only thing tied to dues collected (V12).

Payback, as formulas with the baselines measured in weeks 1–4

  • Cost: 36–39 developer-weeks at your loaded rate; inference rounds to zero.
  • Tickets: list tickets/month × avoidable share (target 30 %) × CS cost per ticket.
  • Admin time: segments/month × minutes saved × admin cost; the client's gain, so it feeds renewal, not margin.
  • Dues: profiles moved to "current" × response uplift × average dues.
  • Breakeven I accept: engineering recovered within four quarters from ticket savings and retained ARR across the pilot and the first 50-tenant wave, before any upsell.

Packaging hypothesis, tested in the pilot

  • Included for all: status, criteria, split counts, duplicate report, score at renewal.
  • Included with fair use: the sentence box, 300 requests/month, soft block and CS alert beyond. Catherine will not pay for a text box (V12).
  • Priced add-on: the reactivation loop, tested on the 3 expert tenants as a flat monthly fee per member tier, a fraction of the dues it demonstrably recovers, reverting to included if reactivation stays under 10 %. Proof on the client's own file: 2 → 26 → 49 → 6 current or to-confirm.

Renewal pack, generated per tenant each quarter

Score and its delta, hours saved, "not me" replies before and after, dues response of current + to-confirm sends vs historical, and an attribution line (reactivations sent → profiles updated → dues recovered) so the uplift is credited to the platform. Isabelle's "prove the value of the network" (V10) as a by-product.

Competitive line

Hivebrite, Graduway and similar market AI search and assistants; to my knowledge none scores a segment's reliability or states in one sentence what leaves the platform (to verify in week 1). Press hypothesis: "an AI that is never sent a profile, hosted in Europe". The sentence box is copyable in a month; a normalised, reliability-scored, consented base per tenant is not.

Annex B · Selection logic (one page) · the brief's request: "graduates working in finance in London for more than three years" · full SQL and 49-row result attached (annexB.sql, annexB_result.csv)
-- Reference date 2026-10-12. Runs as written on SQLite (LIKE only, no REGEXP); for MariaDB replace date(d,'-N years') by DATE_SUB(d, INTERVAL N YEAR).
-- Column names are the dataset's own (French); everything else is English.
-- 0. DEDUPLICATE: file has 525 rows, brief says 500. M0501..M0525 are duplicate persons
--    (capitalised/compound surname, truncated first name, gmail, same job + start date).
CREATE TABLE members AS            -- (preceded by DROP TABLE IF EXISTS in the attached file)
WITH p AS (SELECT *, lower(substr(prenom,1,3)) fn,
            lower(replace(replace(replace(nom,'-',''),' ',''),'.','')) ln FROM profils),
duplicates AS (SELECT b.member_id drop_id FROM p a JOIN p b
   ON a.member_id<>b.member_id AND a.fn=b.fn
  AND (instr(a.ln,b.ln)>0 OR instr(b.ln,a.ln)>0)
  AND a.date_debut_poste=b.date_debut_poste                 -- same start date: Martin is not Martinez
  AND (a.date_maj_profil>b.date_maj_profil OR (a.date_maj_profil=b.date_maj_profil AND a.member_id<b.member_id)))
SELECT * FROM p WHERE member_id NOT IN (SELECT drop_id FROM duplicates);    -- 500 members
-- 1. NORMALISE (deterministic lookup tables, no model, nothing leaves the platform)
CREATE TABLE members_norm AS SELECT m.*,
 CASE WHEN lower(ville) LIKE '%london%' OR lower(ville) LIKE '%londres%'
       OR lower(ville) LIKE '%canary wharf%' THEN 'London' ELSE ville END city_norm,   -- 8 spellings
 CASE WHEN lower(trim(coalesce(pays,''))) IN ('uk','united kingdom','royaume-uni','england','angleterre') THEN 'GB'
      WHEN pays IS NULL AND lower(ville) LIKE '%lond%' THEN 'GB' ELSE pays END country_norm,
 CASE WHEN secteur_declare IN ('Finance','Banque','Finance / Assurance') THEN 'finance:declared'
      WHEN entreprise IN ('Amundi','AXA IM','Barclays','BNP Paribas','Crédit Agricole CIB',
        'Deutsche Bank','Goldman Sachs','HSBC','J.P. Morgan','Lazard','Morgan Stanley',
        'Natixis','Rothschild & Co','Société Générale','UBS') THEN 'finance:employer'
      WHEN lower(poste) LIKE '%financ%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%m&a%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%investment bank%'
        OR lower(poste) LIKE '%trader%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%quant%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%portfolio%'
        OR lower(poste) LIKE '%asset manag%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%opcvm%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%private equity%'
        OR lower(poste) LIKE '%equity research%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%treasury%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%credit risk%'
        OR lower(poste) LIKE '%risk manager%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%contrôleur de gestion%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%fp&a%'
        OR lower(poste) LIKE '%banquier%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%conformité bancaire%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%auditeur financier%'
        OR lower(poste) LIKE '%gestionnaire de portefeuille%' OR lower(poste) LIKE '%directeur financier%' THEN 'finance:title'
      ELSE 'none' END finance_src,                       -- job-title keywords are French because the data is
 -- bounds: A = [0, 2 y) since update OR [0, 1 y) since login; B = [2, 4 y); C = 4 y+ or no date
 CASE WHEN date_maj_profil > date('2026-10-12','-2 years')                             -- current
       OR (derniere_connexion > date('2026-10-12','-1 year') AND derniere_connexion <= '2026-10-12') THEN 'A'
      WHEN date_maj_profil > date('2026-10-12','-4 years') THEN 'B'                    -- to confirm
      ELSE 'C' END reliability                                                          -- to reactivate
FROM members m;
-- 2. SELECT
SELECT member_id, poste, entreprise, finance_src, reliability FROM members_norm
WHERE city_norm='London' AND country_norm='GB'            -- "in London" (country gate: not London, Ontario)
  AND finance_src<>'none'                                 -- "working in finance"
  AND date_debut_poste <= date('2026-10-12','-3 years')    -- "for more than three years"
  AND CAST(promo AS INTEGER) <= 2026;      -- "graduates": all are (class years 2005-2024); CAST survives a text import

Result

49 members (500 after dedup → 168 in London → 72 in finance → 49 with 3+ years in post). Source: 24 declared sector, 17 employer, 8 job keywords. Reliability: Current 1To confirm 5To reactivate 43 · 14 paying. Naive filter (city value 'Londres', sector value 'Finance', 3+ years in post) on the file as delivered: 2. Normalised, no dedup: 26. "6 to e-mail" = current + to confirm; strictly current: 1.

Assumptions

  • Reference date 2026-10-12 (latest non-future date in the file: 2026-10-06).
  • "Graduates" = everyone (class years 2005–2024). The "Master in Finance" diploma is not a finance signal: only 19 of its 47 holders (after dedup) work in finance by any of the three sources.
  • "Finance" = declared sector, else employer in a finance reference list, else job-title keywords. Keywords are the weakest source: 3 of 8 are doubtful ("Professeur de finance" at BCG and at Renault, "Assistant administratif et financier" at Orange); in the product, the NAF industry code comes first and keyword matches are shown "to confirm".
  • "3+ years" = current job started on or before 2023-10-12.
  • Dedup rule (same first-name prefix, surname contained, same start date) drops exactly the 25 apparent duplicates; an earlier "or same job" variant also collapsed Martin into Martinez, which is why production uses a scored candidate list with blocking keys, reviewed by the admin.

What the data cannot verify, and what the product does

  • "In London for 3+ years": no date for the location, only for the job. Job tenure is the proxy, labelled "3+ years in current role" in the UI.
  • "Still true": 43 of 49 profiles are 4+ years old with no login in 2 years. Marked "to reactivate"; a Profile Update Campaign is offered before any send instead of silent inclusion.
