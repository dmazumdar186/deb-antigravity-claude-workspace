# Simplicity rules: over-complication is the enemy of value (adopted 2026-11-09)

Born from losing ASSET Voyages to a competitor who shipped one plain WordPress site while we shipped two platforms, a test-plan hub and seven red-team rounds (`deliverables/asset_voyages_rfp_2026-10-28/30_Postmortem_Lost_to_voyagedemo.md`). Applies to every deliverable and every answer, not only sites.

1. **Build exactly what was asked, in the form named.** If the client says WordPress, it is WordPress. Alternatives, upgrades and "better ways" go in the proposal or a one-line note, never into the deliverable itself.
2. **Show the client their real thing.** A demo is their future site/product with their journeys inside it, on a real neutral domain, not a feature lab, a sandbox tour or a "how to test" page. A report is the answer, not the method.
3. **Zero-friction first click.** One link, loads instantly, needs no instructions, no logins to juggle, no "reload if stuck". If the reader must read a manual to see the value, it is not done.
4. **Harden to the stage.** Demo: credible, safe, fictional data. Production-grade security, isolation, throttles and test batteries belong to the paid build. Spend effort where the buyer can see it.
5. **Red-team substance, not style; cap the loop.** Outward text: at most 2 lens rounds. Only substance blocks (facts, names, figures, dates, prices, commitments, scope, legal, confidentiality). Tone, hedging and wording preference are MINOR advice. Each round must make the text shorter or clearer, never longer and more hedged.
6. **Ship the simple version first, then improve if time remains.** A good thing on time beats a perfect thing late.

Check before shipping anything: "Would a non-technical buyer see the value in 10 seconds from one link, and is this the thing they asked for?" If no, cut until yes.
