# LinkedIn scroll-stop and conversion evidence — fact dossier (2026-10-25)

Scope: what makes a solo B2B founder/consultant post stop the scroll and convert to profile visits and calls, 2025–2026. Every figure carries source, URL, date and sample where stated. Figures that could not be verified against a primary or reputable secondary source are listed under "Not found / could not verify" rather than guessed.

Confidence key: **high** = primary source fetched and read (LinkedIn newsroom/engineering, Gartner, NN/g, study authors' own pages); **med** = large-sample vendor study read on the vendor's own page, or a figure attributed to a paywalled primary by two or more independent secondaries; **low** = single secondary, small sample, or method unclear.

## What this means for a 1080×1350 typographic card from a solo AI consultant

1. Post from the personal profile, not a page: equal engagement rate but ~1.5x median impressions (AuthoredUp 1,006 vs 659) and text reach 0.88x vs 0.42x.
2. 4:5 portrait is the right canvas: portrait single images carry the highest median ER (2.83% vs 2.54% square, AuthoredUp, n=368k) and 72% of activity is mobile (van der Blom 2025).
3. Do not rely on hashtags for traffic: LinkedIn calls the hashtag feed unused and phased out; the only measured effects are zero to negative, and >6 hurts. Use ≤3, topic-exact, or none; put keywords in the caption.
4. The card competes with the format tier above it: documents (7.00%) and multi-image (6.45%) out-engage single images (5.30%) on Socialinsider's 2.45M-post Page data; a single card is the floor, not the ceiling.
5. Typographic/quote cards specifically are flagged as the weakest image type in creator data (Growtempo) — the card must carry information (a number, a chart, a claim), not decoration, and the 800–900-character caption is where reach is earned (1.04x), not the image.
6. Write for dwell: LinkedIn ranks on normalised long-dwell probability (Engineering, Oct 2024); text posts ≥1,000 characters get 1.18x reach, <300 get 0.88x.
7. Never end with "comment X to get Y": such posts fell to −52% impressions vs author average from late July 2026; a genuine closing question held at −4%.
8. Keep links out of the body and preferably out of the post: every placement now sits 16–21% below the same author's link-free posts (MagicPost, since mid-May 2026); let the profile be the link.
9. Cadence 2–5/week, max one per day: Buffer and AuthoredUp agree the big step is 1 → 2–5 per week; AuthoredUp medians fall off at 8+.
10. Expect the long tail, not a golden-hour verdict: 8% of impressions arrive in hour one, 72% by day three; posting hour moves final reach ≤2%, so Tue–Thu mornings CET are a tiebreaker, not a lever.

Conversion expectations the figures support: median post ≈1,000 impressions; 1–3% of impressions becoming profile visits and 0.5–2% of visitors messaging/booking are practitioner ranges only (low confidence) — i.e. roughly one inbound conversation per several thousand impressions. The buyer-side case for the content is strong (73% trust TL over marketing material; 75% researched something new because of it; 60% will pay a premium; 95% of hidden buyers more receptive to outreach), but 95% of the audience is out-of-market, so the card's job is memory, not conversion.
